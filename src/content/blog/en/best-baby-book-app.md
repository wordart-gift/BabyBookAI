## How to choose, in three questions

1. **Will you still be doing it in month eight?** Be honest. A weekly habit is easy in the first month and hard during a sleep regression or back at work. An unfinished book is a common way this ends, on paper and in apps.
2. **Do you want your words or your photos at the center?** Prompt apps are better at saving what you would otherwise forget: what you were feeling, the funny thing that happened. A camera-roll app is better at showing the whole year.
3. **What do you want to hold at the end?** A binder that grows as you go, a large book with many pages, or a shorter book of chosen photos are different objects. Look at real photos of each.

## The apps that ask for a few minutes a week

**The Short Years** suits parents who like a clear routine. Its pages arrive in installments, which gives you something real to hold along the way. It is a binder with rings rather than a bound book, and the price covers printing and shipping with no subscription on its shop page.

**Baby Notebook** is the one to look at for a large format. It offers 12 × 12″ books with more than 100 pages, and you print once your baby turns one. The Plus and Premium plans change what the book costs, so work out the total for the months you expect to stay subscribed.

**Qeepsake** asks the least of you among the prompt apps, since you can answer from your text messages. Before you commit, find out what the printed book will cost, because the listing does not state it.

**Baby Diaries** leans toward writing. You set your own writing moments rather than answering a fixed list of questions, and it offers a choice of layouts. Its price list is the hardest of the five to read, so see what you would pay for the printed copy in the app before you begin.

## The app that works from the photos you already have

[BabyBookAI](page:home) takes the opposite approach. You give it your baby's name and a start date, and it reads the camera roll on your phone. It drops the blurry shots, keeps the sharpest of each burst, sorts the rest by month and lays out every page. You can swap any photo, edit the words and add a note before you order.

Its limits are real, and you should know them:

- **It has no prompts or journal.** It will not remind you to write anything down, so the small stories stay in your head unless you add them at the end.
- **It only knows what your phone holds.** Months with few photos make thinner spreads.
- **The book is smaller.** It has 30 to 40 pages and covers 6, 12 or 18 months, so it is a selection rather than a record of everything.
- **There is one format.** The book is always a square {size} hardcover.

It suits parents who already take plenty of photos and know a weekly habit will not last. There is more detail on the [baby photo book](page:babyPhotoBook) and the [first year photo book](page:firstYear).

## And if you would rather have paper

A fill-in baby book needs no app at all, and your handwriting becomes part of the keepsake. It asks the most of you: a pen, printed photos and glue, every month. If you love that kind of thing, nothing here replaces it.

For everyone else, the best baby book app is the one you will finish.
